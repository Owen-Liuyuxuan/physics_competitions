By Jaan Kalda
(TalTech).
The space between two parallel infinite metallic plates is filled with homogeneous material of heat conductivity
\kappa
. The heat conductivity of the metallic plates is much larger than
\kappa
, the distance between the plates is
L
. One of the plates is kept at temperature
T_1
, and the other – at temperature
T_2
. There is a spherical cavity of radius
R
in the medium between the plates, at equal distance
L/2
from the both plates; assume that
R\ll L
.  Initially, the cavity is filled with a gas of negligibly small heat conductivity (much smaller than
\kappa
). By how much will change the total heat flux
P
between the plates (measured in watts) if the spherical cavity is filled with a material of very large heat conductivity (much larger than
\kappa
)?
