Problem 2 – Phase transitions in a wire
In total, 91 solutions were submitted for problem 2, out of which 25  were correct.
Congratulations to everyone who managed to solve this problem!
The Best Solution Awards
were selected from the 7 submissions sent before the second hint was published. Usually, only those solutions are considered that are not based on any hints, but this time, there were too few such solutions, and as a matter of fact, the first hint was very subtle. The Best Solution Award was shared between the solutions that analyzed in detail the heat transfer along the wire near the phase boundary. There were two such solutions, submitted by
Luka Passek-Kumerički
and
Anindya Guria
. Both of them derive the temperature profile along the wire, but
Luka Passek-Kumerički
is the only one who correctly shows the rapid process of power rising from
P_0
to
\sqrt{\frac 23}P_0
and to
\sqrt{\frac 83}P_0
on the graph. Everyone else treats this as a discontinuity which is not correct: the thermalization is fast as compared to the voltage growth time, but not instantaneous. As a result,
Luka Passek-Kumerički
earns
0.6
of the award, and
Anindya Guria
– the remaining
0.4.
For my taste (JK),  Luka Passek-Kumerički has unnecessarily complicated calculations, e.g. his formulae 1-25 and 38; still, these are not wrong, so I overlook this.
Additionally, very well-written solutions were submitted by
Vlad-Stefan Oros
,
Eyal Walach
, and
Gil Ronen
(note that E.W. has a typo in Section 1.3, instead of
T_h-T_c=T_c-T_h
, there should be
T_h-T_c=T_c-T_l
). These are well worth reading as they explain in an easy-to-understand way how the phase separation front propagates. All these authors get a publication bonus factor of exp(0.1).
